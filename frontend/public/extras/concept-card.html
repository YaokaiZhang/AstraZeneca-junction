Ajoissa: equal cancer care,
in every county.

From screening gaps to action plans, county by county. A working tool that shows each of Finland's 22 screening areas where people are lost in bowel, breast and cervical cancer screening, and writes a one-page plan to fix it.

The problem

The National Cancer Strategy 2026–2035 (p. 5) asks for 80% screening participation in every county (Goal 2) and treatment within target times (Goal 3), with regional differences used to steer services. The Cancer Registry publishes county results every year, but the gaps sit at different steps in different places, and nothing turns the tables into a plan.

Finnish Cancer Registry, 2024 round.

How it works

1See the chain

Three steps per programme, county against Finland: take part → follow-up after an abnormal result → wait for the diagnostic test. On track Behind Check data

2Start with one action

One proven action for the weakest step: who does it, how often, what it achieves (a study's effect applied to the county, labelled an estimate), what it takes a week, how to measure it.

3Print the plan

The AI writes a four-line briefing from our figures and approved sources; every number and citation is checked. One A4 page with charts, for the county team.

Example · Pohjois-Savo, bowel cancer screening (2024)

Action: the screening nurse gets a daily list of everyone with a positive test and no colonoscopy booked, and calls them (patient navigation, risk ratio 1.24).

Why you can trust it

  • The AI only writes words; every number comes from our calculations.
  • It may cite only 14 reviewed sources; a fixed template is used if any check fails.
  • Reporting gaps are not called missed care: Varsinais-Suomi, 4 of 190 colposcopies on record, shows check data.
  • Our warning lines are labelled as ours; only the 80% goal is official.
  • Public, aggregate data only. No personal data.

Who it is for

  • County screening leads and health centres: the monitor and the one-page plan.
  • Residents: the same figures in plain words, who each programme is for, and where to ask.

Strategy link

Goal 2: 80% participation. Goal 3: treatment within target times; time to treatment is not published by county, so the follow-up and wait steps are the closest public figures.
